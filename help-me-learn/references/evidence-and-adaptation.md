# Evidence and adaptation

This skill is an educational workflow, not a clinical intervention or diagnostic tool. Its design draws on the supplied research notes and output-style example. Those documents have not been independently fact-checked in this package.

## What was retained

The community research notes emphasize individual differences, low-friction starting steps, interactive practice, external progress records, and easy re-entry. These inform configurable product behavior. They do not establish that any method works for every person with ADHD.

The longer framework inspired source triage, worked applications, and visual explanations. Its broad biological explanations, precise timing claims, and product recommendations are not adopted as verified facts. The example `i-have-adhd` skill inspired visible next actions and concise formatting, but not statements such as everything off-screen being forgotten or a global mode that persists across unrelated tasks.

## Defaults, not findings

One question per turn, 3–5 questions per chapter, a short visible roadmap, and optional time blocks are design defaults. Adjust them to the learner and task. They are not clinically validated prescriptions. Do not infer that a preference for visuals defines a fixed learning style, or that short responses imply limited ability.

## When evidence is requested

Use primary research and authoritative guidance, checking population, study design, comparison, measured outcome, and limitations. Separate evidence about general learning from evidence specifically about ADHD. Label anecdotes and hypotheses. Do not turn an association into a mechanism or promise that the skill treats ADHD. If sources cannot be checked, say so and avoid invented citations.

Keep any future evidence additions concise: claim, supporting source, scope/limitations, verification date, and what instruction it changes. No external app or paid service is required by this skill.
