# Assessment: Generating question sets

## When to use this reference

Read when creating chapter questions or assessing a custom topic. [Assessment](assessment.md) covers collection, grading, and evidence. These adjustable patterns do not prescribe test length or prove mastery.

## Base pattern: 3–5 questions per outcome set

Aim for one or two angles per taught outcome within a manageable set. A question may target two related outcomes if its criteria distinguish their evidence.

| Type | What it checks | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **E — Explanation** | Restate an idea in the learner's own words. | “What does the numerator tell you?” |
| **A — Application** | Use the concept in a fresh concrete case. | “Represent 3/5 with a rectangle. Explain your choices.” |
| **T — Transfer or misconception** | Handle a boundary, changed condition, or plausible mistake. | “Is 5/3 greater than 1? Explain your reasoning.” |
| **W — Worked reverse** | Explain or reconstruct the reasoning behind a supplied solution. | “Why does 1/2 + 1/4 equal 3/4? Explain the first step.” |

Worked reverse reveals the answer to assess justification, not independent solution production. Record the help supplied.

## Map outcomes to questions

| Questions per outcome | Pattern | Appropriate scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | E or A | Quick diagnostic; leave other angles unassessed. |
| 2 | E + A | Definition and use. |
| 2–3 | E + A + T | Deeper coverage including a misconception. |
| 3+ | Add W where useful | Extended reasoning practice; retention still needs a later check. |

A five-question set might use Q1 E and Q2 A for outcome 1, Q3 E/A and Q4 T for outcome 2, and Q5 E for outcome 3. This tests **three outcomes**; outcome 3 lacks application evidence. For large chapters, split assessment or state what remains unassessed.

## Sequence without giving away answers

Start with an accessible explanation or application, then deepen the challenge. Place misconceptions after the baseline check when appropriate. Check for answer leakage: one question's definition or solution may answer another. One-at-a-time presentation reduces forward hints but cannot prevent earlier questions priming later answers.

Avoid: “The numerator, which counts selected pieces, tells you what?” Prefer: “What does the numerator tell you?” Use “Explain your reasoning” or “Show the steps” when the outcome requires more than a final answer.

## Prepare acceptance criteria first

For each question, record its outcome IDs, core insight, acceptable wording or representations, partial-credit boundary, and likely misconceptions. Keep this key separate from learner-facing prompts. A local agent-notes file is not secret storage.

For fractions, watch for swapped numerator/denominator meanings, unequal pieces, or assuming every fraction is below one. A numerator **can** be zero; a denominator cannot. Accept valid visual, verbal, numeric, or alternative procedural answers when they demonstrate the intended outcome.

Link inspected source IDs and locators using the [extraction schema](state-schema.md#resource-extraction-schema). Check the key against the source before evaluating; correct a flawed question or key openly.

## Custom topics and domain patterns

For an unprepared topic, list taught outcomes, select E or A questions, and build a short set. Clarify ambiguous answers with a follow-up. Do not introduce new material to fill a question quota.

| Domain | E | A | T | W |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Math or coding | Describe the mechanism | Solve a fresh problem | Change a constraint | Justify a worked step |
| History, philosophy, design | State the claim | Apply it to a case | Examine a counterargument | Reconstruct the argument |
| Writing or composition | Identify a technique | Try it in a short piece | Explain when it fails | Analyze how an example works |
